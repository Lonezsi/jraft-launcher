New worlds

Worlds worth the walk.

Mountains rise into real ranges with valleys and rivers between them. Badlands split into deep canyons, volcanoes glow on the skyline, and dozens of new biomes sit between the old ones. The Nether and the End are rebuilt too, with new biomes, fortresses and towering floating islands. Ruins, watchtowers and graveyards turn up along the way, so there's always something on the next hill.

Distant Horizons

See all the way to the mountains.

Past your normal view distance the land keeps going, drawn in lighter detail so the game stays smooth. Climb a hill and the whole valley is there instead of a wall of fog. Far terrain fills in quietly as you play, and it matches the server's world because both run the same world mods.

Light and atmosphere

Light you can feel.

Sunsets turn lakes to gold and long shadows stretch across the grass. Fog settles in the forest, rain leaves puddles, and the night sky fills with stars. Hold a torch and it lights the path in your hand. Leaves drift down from the trees, dust hangs in the air of old caves, and waterfalls throw up spray where they land.

Motion and sound

Everything moves. Everything sounds.

Alive in every frame.

Villagers gesture as they talk, cats stretch, wolves wag and llamas look around. Look down and you see your own body. Skins have real depth, the camera leans into turns, and swinging, eating and drinking all animate in first person. Elytra bank as you fly and leave trails behind you.

Listen closely.

Footsteps change with the ground: grass rustles, planks knock, gravel and snow crunch. Forests, beaches and caves each have their own background sound. Sound echoes through big caves, goes muffled behind walls, and water drips from the cave ceiling above you.

Nature and 3D packs

Lusher. Rounder. Deeper.

Grass grows tall in many shades, with clovers, tiny flowers and dry tufts mixed in. Bushes fill out, leaves become soft round canopies, and every flower and sapling is redrawn. What the launcher does

Open it. You're playing.

The JRAFT app does all the setup a modpack usually asks of you, and checks it again every time you open it.

Straight onto the server.

The game starts and joins the JRAFT server for you, voice chat included, through the app's own secure tunnel. JRAFT also sits at the top of your server list. The app stays open while you play. Prism closes once the game is running and quits when you leave.

One app for your computer.

Windows, Mac (Apple silicon and Intel) and Linux. On Windows it installs itself with desktop and Start menu shortcuts. On Windows and Linux it updates itself from each new release when it starts; on a Mac the log tells you when a new version is out.

Works with Prism Launcher.

Already have Prism? It uses yours, and your accounts and other instances stay as they are. No Prism? It downloads a portable copy and keeps it updated. Either way you get a JRAFT instance with the JRAFT icon.

Brings its own Java.

It downloads Java 25 just for JRAFT, so nothing else on your PC changes, and gives the game memory to match: from 2.5 GB on small machines up to 6 GB with 24 GB of RAM or more.

Every file checked.

Mods, resource packs and shaders come from Modrinth, CurseForge and the JRAFT repository. Each one is checked against its sha512 fingerprint, and a busy server gets a few more tries. Files it installed that have left the pack are removed.

Your settings stay yours.

Default configs and options are copied only when they're missing, so your keybinds and tweaks survive updates. A couple of fixes are re-applied once when the pack changes them. It never deletes your files: a mod you added that can't run here moves to mods-disabled.

Packs in the right order.

All 37 resource packs are switched on in the order they need to be in. Packs you added yourself stay on, just below the top one.

Fixes packs on your PC.

It removes broken item models, like tools and shields, from Fresh Flowers and Plants and Fresh Food, because their licence doesn't allow sharing a fixed copy. Two JRAFT packs merge seven grass packs into one and put Os' Colorful Leaves' textures on Better Leaves' bushy shape.

Tuned to your graphics card.

It reads your graphics card and picks one of four presets: view distance, far terrain and shaders, set so the game runs well on that card.

Picks your account.

More than one Microsoft account in Prism? Choose who's playing and tick Don't ask again. A Switch account button shows for a moment as the game starts.

Easy to get help.

Show log opens what the app did, step by step, and Copy log puts it on your clipboard to send. If something goes wrong it says so in red and tells you what to send.

How to play

Ready when you are.

  1. Download the app.

    Pick your computer below. On a Mac, unzip it and move JRAFT to Applications.

  2. Open it every time.

    That's how you get updates. It finds or brings Prism, adds the JRAFT instance and downloads the pack.

  3. Sign in once.

    The first time, sign in with your Microsoft account in Prism's window. After that it remembers you.

  4. You're in.

    The game starts and joins the JRAFT server, voice chat included.
